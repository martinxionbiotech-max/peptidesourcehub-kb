## Research Background

Epithalon (Ala-Glu-Asp-Gly) is a synthetic tetrapeptide designed by Prof. Vladimir Khavinson at the Saint Petersburg Institute of Bioregulation and Gerontology. Derived from epithalamin — a polypeptide complex extracted from bovine pineal glands — through structure-activity optimization to identify the minimal active sequence. The resulting tetrapeptide has become one of the most studied peptide bioregulators in longevity research, with published data demonstrating telomerase activation via hTERT gene expression and telomere length preservation.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q1: What is the evidence for Epithalon's effect on telomeres?

Khavinson et al. demonstrated that Epithalon activates hTERT gene expression in human somatic cell cultures, leading to telomere length maintenance and extended replicative lifespan. The proposed mechanism involves chromatin remodeling at the hTERT promoter region.

### Q2: How does Epithalon relate to epithalamin?

Epithalamin is a crude polypeptide complex from bovine pineal glands. Epithalon (Ala-Glu-Asp-Gly) is the synthetic tetrapeptide capturing the active sequence identified through systematic fragmentation of epithalamin.

### Q3: Is Epithalon the same as Epitalon?

Yes. 'Epithalon,' 'Epitalon,' and 'AEDG peptide' all refer to the same Ala-Glu-Asp-Gly tetrapeptide. Name variations arise from transliteration differences from Russian (Эпиталон).

### Q4: What is the pineal peptide hypothesis of aging?

The hypothesis proposes that the pineal gland produces peptide bioregulators coordinating circadian rhythms, neuroendocrine function, and cellular maintenance. Age-related pineal decline → reduced signaling → accelerated aging. Epithalon is proposed to restore this signaling deficiency.

### Q5: What purity specification applies to Epithalon?

≥99% by HPLC at 214 nm. As a simple tetrapeptide, synthesis is straightforward but the C-terminal glycine requires careful handling to avoid racemization. ESI-MS and amino acid analysis in every COA.
