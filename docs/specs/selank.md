## Research Background

Selank is a synthetic heptapeptide sharing the same Pro-Gly-Pro C-terminal extension design as Semax. While Semax derives from ACTH, Selank derives from tuftsin (Thr-Lys-Pro-Arg) — a natural immunomodulatory tetrapeptide. The Pro-Gly-Pro tail transforms tuftsin from a pure immune peptide into a dual-function molecule engaging the immune-neural interface while enabling CNS penetration. Selank's primary research application is anxiolysis through GABAergic and dopaminergic modulation — producing anxiety reduction without sedation, cognitive impairment, or dependence liability.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q1: How does Selank compare to benzodiazepines?

Selank produces anxiolytic effects through GABAergic modulation without sedation, amnesia, tolerance, or withdrawal. Rather than directly agonizing GABA-A, Selank modulates endogenous GABA tone — a more subtle mechanism preserving cognitive function and alertness.

### Q2: What is the connection between tuftsin and Selank?

Tuftsin (Thr-Lys-Pro-Arg) is a natural splenic tetrapeptide that activates macrophages. Selank adds Pro-Gly-Pro to tuftsin, creating dual functionality: immunomodulation + CNS delivery. This immune-neural bridge is a defining feature of Selank's pharmacology.

### Q3: How does Selank differ from Semax?

Both are PGP-extended heptapeptides but from different parents. Semax (ACTH-derived) → melanocortin → BDNF/NGF → cognition. Selank (tuftsin-derived) → GABA/dopamine → anxiolysis. Complementary: Semax for cognition, Selank for anxiety.

### Q4: Does Selank cause sedation?

No — a significant distinction from benzodiazepines. In animal models, Selank produces anxiolytic effects at doses that do not cause sedation, motor impairment, or amnesia. Attributed to modulation of endogenous GABA tone rather than direct receptor agonism.
