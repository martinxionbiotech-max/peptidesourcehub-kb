## Biological Context

Thymosin Beta-4 (Tβ4) is a 43-amino acid polypeptide (MW ~4,921 Da) present in most mammalian tissues. It was originally identified as a thymic hormone but is now recognized as the principal G-actin sequestering peptide in eukaryotic cells. The synthetic fragment TB-500 corresponds to the N-terminal actin-binding domain of Tβ4 (residues 17–23, LKKTETQ), which is essential for its actin-polymerization regulatory functions.

The fragment notation "17–23" refers to the amino acid positions within the full Tβ4 sequence, not molecular weight or any other parameter.

## Special Notes

- **FOR LABORATORY RESEARCH USE ONLY.**
- TB-500 is a synthetic fragment and is not isolated from biological sources. The product does not contain thymic tissue extracts or any biologically derived raw materials.
- The G-actin binding affinity of the LKKTETQ fragment is retained at non-physiological pH (5.5–8.5 range), but experimental protocols should consider buffer conditions for optimal activity.
- As a short linear peptide, TB-500 is susceptible to non-specific adsorption to plastic labware. Use siliconized or low-retention vials and pipette tips for dilute solutions.

## Stability & Storage

| Condition | Degradation Profile |
|---|---|
| **Neutral pH (5.5–8.5)** | Stable; the actin-binding affinity of the LKKTETQ fragment is retained across this broad pH range |
| **Repeated Freeze-Thaw** | Avoid; each freeze-thaw cycle increases risk of peptide degradation, aggregation, and loss of activity. Aliquot into single-use portions after initial reconstitution |
| **Acidic pH (<4.0)** | Potential hydrolysis of peptide bonds; avoid strongly acidic buffers |
| **Alkaline pH (>9.0)** | Risk of deamidation at Gln residue; degradation accelerates |
| **Temperature** | Lyophilized: stable 24 months at 2–8°C, 36 months at −20°C. Reconstituted: 24 h at 4°C; freeze at −20°C for ≤30 days |
| **Light Exposure** | Protect from light; store in amber vials or opaque containers |
