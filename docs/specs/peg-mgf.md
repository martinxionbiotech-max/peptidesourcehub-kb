## Research Background

PEG-MGF (PEGylated Mechano Growth Factor) is a modified form of IGF-1Ec — the splice variant of IGF-1 preferentially expressed in skeletal muscle in response to mechanical load. MGF differs from systemic IGF-1 by a 24-amino acid C-terminal E-domain insert that confers distinct receptor binding and specifically activates muscle satellite (stem) cells. PEGylation extends the peptide's half-life from minutes to ~24 hours, making it viable for chronic muscle regeneration research.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q1: What is Mechano Growth Factor (MGF)?

MGF is the IGF-1Ec splice variant expressed locally in skeletal muscle following mechanical load or damage. It contains a 24-amino acid E-domain that targets muscle satellite cells specifically. The 'mechano' prefix refers to its induction by mechanical stress.

### Q2: Why is PEGylation necessary for MGF?

Native MGF has a very short half-life (minutes). PEGylation increases hydrodynamic radius, reduces renal clearance, and extends half-life to ~24 hours. Without PEGylation, MGF would not be viable for most research protocols.

### Q3: How does MGF differ from standard IGF-1?

(1) MGF is a local splice variant expressed in muscle, not liver-derived; (2) the E-domain insert confers satellite cell receptor specificity; (3) MGF's primary role is stem cell activation/myoblast proliferation, while systemic IGF-1 primarily drives protein synthesis in mature myofibers.

### Q4: What research applications use PEG-MGF?

Satellite cell (muscle stem cell) biology, muscle regeneration/repair following injury models, sarcopenia research, and exercise physiology studies exploring the MGF response to mechanical loading.

### Q5: What QC is unique to PEG-MGF?

Beyond standard HPLC and MS: SDS-PAGE confirms PEGylation band shift; LAL endotoxin testing; NMR or colorimetric PEG verification. All in batch-specific COA.
